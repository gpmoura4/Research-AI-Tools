# Discrepancy and Disliking Do Not Induce Negative Opinion Shifts

- **Citation key**: Takacs_2016
- **Objective**: To test experimentally whether opinion discrepancy with, or disliking of, a source of influence produces negative social influence, defined as shifts away from the opinion of others.
- **Method**: Two laboratory studies in a controlled computer-mediated setting: one with controlled exposure to others' opinions, one with opinion exchange and a disliking treatment, varying initial opinion distance and liking of the source.
- **Key findings**:
  - Similarity induced attraction, as expected.
  - Neither large opinion discrepancy nor disliking produced negative opinion shifts; the results fit a positive linear relationship between opinion distance and opinion shift.
  - Earlier evidence for negative influence suffers from design weaknesses (group cues, perceived rather than measured distance, uncontrolled opinion trends).
  - The authors do not exclude negative influence under other conditions and call for identifying its moderators.
- **Relevance to theme**: Defines "negative social influence" explicitly (abstract) and provides the strongest experimental evidence against it, which bears on both the name and the evidence rating of FAMO mechanism B7.
- **Keywords**: negative influence; opinion shifts; bi-polarization; disliking; laboratory experiment [inferred]

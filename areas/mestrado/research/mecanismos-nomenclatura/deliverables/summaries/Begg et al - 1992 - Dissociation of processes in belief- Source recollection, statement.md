# Dissociation of Processes in Belief: Source Recollection, Statement Familiarity, and the Illusion of Truth

- **Citation key**: Begg_1992
- **Objective**: To separate the contributions of source recollection and statement familiarity to the effect of repetition on rated truth, which the authors call the illusory-truth effect.
- **Method**: Four experiments in which statements were paired with sources of differing credibility (labelled true or false) and later rated for truth, with estimates of the independent contributions of recollection and familiarity.
- **Key findings**:
  - Repeated statements attributed to a "false" source were still rated truer than new statements, unless the sources were especially memorable.
  - Recollection of the source is a controlled influence; familiarity is an automatic one.
  - Impairing control reduced the influence of recollection, while the influence of familiarity stayed constant.
  - The article states that the illusory-truth effect was first observed by Hasher, Goldstein, and Toppino (1977).
- **Relevance to theme**: Earliest source among those read that names the "illusory-truth effect" and attributes its first observation to Hasher et al. (1977), the source cited for FAMO mechanism C7.
- **Keywords**: illusory truth; familiarity; source recollection; belief; repetition [inferred]

# Digital astroturfing in politics: Definition, typology, and countermeasures

- **Citation key**: Kovic_2018
- **Objective**: To define digital astroturfing in politics and lay out a conceptual map of the phenomenon.
- **Method**: Conceptual analysis: a definition with five necessary conditions, a typology, the concept of astroturfing repertoires, and a discussion of countermeasures.
- **Key findings**:
  - Digital astroturfing is defined as manufactured, deceptive, and strategic top-down activity on the Internet, initiated by political actors, that mimics bottom-up activity by autonomous individuals.
  - The authors argue that digital astroturfing differs conceptually from regular astroturfing. They prefer "digital" to the "online astroturfing" of an earlier definition (Zhang et al., 2013).
  - The typology has three dimensions (target, actor type, goals); repertoires combine tools, venues, and actions.
  - Restrictive and incentivizing countermeasures are proposed, and empirical research, though hard, is called feasible.
- **Relevance to theme**: Candidate defining source for renaming FAMO mechanism G3. Its definition is restricted to political actors, which is narrower than G3's scope (coordinated actors, including fake trends).
- **Keywords**: political communication; Internet; public opinion; astroturfing; bots

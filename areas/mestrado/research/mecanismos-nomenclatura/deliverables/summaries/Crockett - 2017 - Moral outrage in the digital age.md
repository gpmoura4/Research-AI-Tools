# Moral outrage in the digital age

- **Citation key**: Crockett_2017
- **Objective**: To propose a psychological framework for how digital media may change the expression of moral outrage and its social consequences.
- **Method**: Comment article. It builds a stimuli → responses → outcomes framework and illustrates it with data from a study of everyday moral experience and prior research.
- **Key findings**:
  - Moral outrage is an emotion elicited by stimuli appraised as moral norm violations, and it motivates shaming and punishment.
  - People are more likely to learn about immoral acts online than in person or through traditional media.
  - Digital media may inflate the triggers of outrage, lower its personal costs, and amplify its personal benefits, while reducing its benefits for society.
- **Relevance to theme**: Defines moral outrage and frames its online expression. This supports naming the object of FAMO mechanism E1 "moral outrage", with Brady et al. (2021) supplying the social-learning process.

# Models of Social Influence: Towards the Next Frontiers

- **Citation key**: Flache_2017
- **Objective**: To review formal models that explain why opinion diversity persists although social influence usually reduces differences between people (Axelrod's question), and to set an agenda for the field.
- **Method**: Conceptual review of three classes of social-influence models (assimilative, similarity-biased, and repulsive influence), their theoretical motivations, critical conditions, and limitations.
- **Key findings**:
  - Repulsive influence (also called rejection, negative influence, differentiation, or reactance) assumes that interaction with very dissimilar others increases opinion differences; models with it can produce bi-polarization (¶2.47–2.50).
  - Bi-polarization in these models depends on critical conditions, including whether both assimilation and differentiation occur and on network structure (long-range ties can trigger repulsive influence).
  - The main limitation of this model class is the lack of empirical work demonstrating the micro-process of repulsive influence; recent experiments failed to find it consistently (¶2.64).
  - The field needs model comparison and far more empirical testing of micro-assumptions and macro-predictions.
- **Relevance to theme**: Defining source for the FAMO mechanism B7. It shows that "negative influence" is one of several labels for repulsive influence, and that its empirical base is weak.
- **Keywords**: social influence; opinion dynamics; repulsive influence; bi-polarization; agent-based models [inferred]

# Knowledge Does Not Protect Against Illusory Truth

- **Citation key**: Fazio_2015
- **Objective**: To test the assumption that prior knowledge constrains the illusory truth effect, the tendency to rate repeated statements as truer than new ones.
- **Method**: Two experiments. Experiment 1 used normed knowledge estimates and a post-experimental knowledge check; Experiment 2 used multinomial modelling of reliance on fluency versus knowledge.
- **Key findings**:
  - Illusory truth effects occurred even for statements that contradicted participants' demonstrated knowledge.
  - Participants sometimes relied on processing fluency even when knowledge was available ("knowledge neglect").
  - The article treats "illusory truth effect" as the established name of the phenomenon and dates the research line to Hasher, Goldstein, and Toppino's (1977) seminal study.
- **Relevance to theme**: Recent, widely cited source that uses the label of FAMO mechanism C7 and shows that repetition can make false partisan claims seem true even to people who know better.
- **Keywords**: illusory truth; fluency; knowledge neglect; repetition; misinformation [inferred]

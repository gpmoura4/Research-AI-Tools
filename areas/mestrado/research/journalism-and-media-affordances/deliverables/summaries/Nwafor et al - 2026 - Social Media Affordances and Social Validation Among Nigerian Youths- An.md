# Social Media Affordances and Social Validation Among Nigerian Youths: An Examination of Self-Presentation and Online Engagement

- **Citation key**: Nwafor_2026
- **Objective**: To examine how perceived social media affordances, self-presentation, and online engagement jointly shape perceived social validation among Nigerian youths.
- **Method**: Online survey of 480 active users aged 18–35 (purposive sampling through Facebook, Instagram, X, and WhatsApp groups), using adapted affordance, self-presentation, and engagement scales, with regression and sequential mediation analysis.
- **Key findings**:
  - Perceived affordances predicted self-presentation (β = 0.79), which predicted engagement (β = 0.43), which predicted perceived validation.
  - Self-presentation and engagement sequentially mediated the link between affordances and validation, with a non-significant direct path.
  - The authors keep platform possibilities, user practices, and outcomes as distinct constructs to avoid conflating them.
- **Relevance to theme**: Offers a non-Western empirical example of an affordance-to-practice-to-outcome chain in which affordances act indirectly, consistent with the FAMO reading of affordances as enabling conditions; the outcome studied is social validation, not polarization.
- **Keywords**: social media affordances; self-presentation; online engagement; social validation; Nigerian youths

# Revisiting Agenda-Setting Across Media Systems and Regime Types: Levels of Influence, the Need for Orientation, and the Algorithmic Turn in a Networked, Polarized Age

- **Citation key**: Almakaty_2026
- **Objective**: To connect three parallel traditions (the three levels of agenda-setting, the Need for Orientation, and comparative media-system and regime research) into a multilevel model for an algorithmically mediated, polarized environment.
- **Method**: Critical integrative review (Torraco) of 108 sources assembled through structured searches and citation tracing, with three illustrative cases (Türkiye, Hungary, India) chosen on a most-different-systems logic.
- **Key findings**:
  - Agenda-setting has moved from a linear transfer of salience to a recursive, algorithmically mediated process; legacy media still set the aggregate agenda.
  - Recent platform experiments (chronological feed, reduced like-minded exposure) changed exposure but not polarization; curation affects salience and association more than attitudes.
  - In Türkiye, network agenda-setting on refugees transferred only to pro-government respondents.
  - Six testable propositions, including "algorithmic orientation" and media-system conditioning of convergent versus divergent effects.
- **Relevance to theme**: Bears on algorithmic agenda-setting, contextual moderators (media system and regime type), and the reliance of platform evidence on U.S. samples, which the FAMO manuscript discusses as a limitation.
- **Keywords**: agenda-setting; network agenda-setting; need for orientation; media systems; hybrid regimes; algorithmic gatekeeping

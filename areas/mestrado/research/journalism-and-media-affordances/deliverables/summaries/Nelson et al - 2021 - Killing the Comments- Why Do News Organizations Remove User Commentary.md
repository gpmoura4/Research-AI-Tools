# Killing the Comments: Why Do News Organizations Remove User Commentary Functions?

- **Citation key**: Nelson_2021
- **Objective**: To identify the reasons news organizations give for removing, limiting, or outsourcing user comment functions.
- **Method**: Thematic analysis (automated text analysis plus modified grounded theory in NVivo) of 20 public statements and policies from U.S. and U.K. news organizations, collected in 2019.
- **Key findings**:
  - The most common rationale was reducing incivility and misinformation in comments, together with the cost of moderation.
  - Organizations redirected discussion to social media, mainly Facebook and Twitter, outsourcing moderation and liability.
  - Statements reflected no consideration of the power this shift transfers to platform companies.
  - Removing or relegating comments is framed as an enactment of journalistic gatekeeping authority.
- **Relevance to theme**: Shows newsrooms moving discussion spaces from their own control into platform governance, which bears on the FAMO practical implications for comment spaces and on platform dependence in journalism.
- **Keywords**: journalism; user comments; incivility; comment feature removal; online news

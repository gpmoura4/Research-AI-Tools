# Synthetic Social Alienation: The Role of Algorithm-Driven Content in Shaping Digital Discourse and User Perspectives

- **Citation key**: Serttas_2025
- **Objective**: To examine how algorithm-driven content curation shapes mediated discourse and user experience, proposing the concept of Synthetic Social Alienation (SSA).
- **Method**: Ten semi-structured interviews with frequent platform users, discourse analysis of 1,500 trending comments from Twitter, TikTok, and YouTube, and sentiment classifiers trained on a hybrid dataset (90 original plus 90 synthetic examples).
- **Key findings**:
  - Four SSA patterns: algorithmic manipulation, digital alienation, platform dependency, and echo chamber effects.
  - Users describe repetitive content loops, limited agency, and polarized emotional framing; professionals and activists report dependence on algorithmic visibility.
  - The paper distinguishes filter bubbles (algorithmic, passive) from echo chambers (socially constructed, active).
  - It proposes algorithmic transparency, content diversity mandates, and critical digital literacy as interventions.
- **Relevance to theme**: Offers a conceptual distinction between filter bubble and echo chamber that matches the FAMO separation of algorithmic curation from network homophily; its evidentiary base is small and partly synthetic.
- **Keywords**: algorithmic alienation; Synthetic Social Alienation; echo chambers; ideological silos; sentiment analysis

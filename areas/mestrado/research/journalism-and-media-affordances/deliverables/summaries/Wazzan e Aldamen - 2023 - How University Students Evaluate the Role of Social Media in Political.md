# How University Students Evaluate the Role of Social Media in Political Polarization: Perspectives of a Sample of Turkish Undergraduate and Graduate Students

- **Citation key**: Wazzan_2023
- **Objective**: To assess whether Turkish university students perceive a link between social media use and political polarization, including echo chambers, filter bubbles, and the spiral of silence.
- **Method**: Online questionnaire (N = 303, convenience and snowball sampling, April–May 2022) and one focus group with six students.
- **Key findings**:
  - Students perceived a weak-to-moderate effect of social media on political polarization.
  - Most do not rely on social media for political news and do not follow political leaders; trust in social media news is low.
  - Algorithms were perceived to contribute to filter bubbles to a medium degree; Twitter showed the most homogeneous followed accounts.
  - Students avoid expressing political views online, mainly for fear that posts are censored, consistent with a spiral of silence.
- **Relevance to theme**: Provides non-U.S. evidence on perceived platform effects, self-censorship, and the spiral of silence, relevant to the FAMO discussion of contextual moderators and of the Anglosphere bias of the evidence base.
- **Keywords**: political polarization; social media; Türkiye; echo chambers; filter bubbles; spiral of silence

# Reconceptualizing Gatekeeping in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: A Theoretical Exploration of Artificial Intelligence-Driven News Curation and Automated Journalism

- **Citation key**: Voinea_2025
- **Objective**: To reconceptualize gatekeeping theory for news environments in which algorithmic curation and LLM-based news generation share the selection, writing, and distribution of news with human editors.
- **Method**: Theoretical synthesis of roughly 80 works selected thematically from communication, journalism, computer science, STS, and AI ethics literature, organized into four themes (recommenders, automated writing, third-party control, feedback loops).
- **Key findings**:
  - Gatekeeping becomes a hybrid, networked human–AI process; optimization goals (engagement vs. diversity) act as de facto editorial criteria.
  - Algorithmic curation can either reinforce mainstream agendas (Nechushtai and Lewis) or introduce new biases; the paper proposes "gatekeeping symmetry" to capture this.
  - Reliance on third-party AI and platforms shifts gatekeeping power away from newsrooms (lock-in, rent extraction).
  - Adaptive algorithms create feedback loops, including confirmation-bias loops and "algorithmic aversion loops" that progressively deprioritize news.
- **Relevance to theme**: Directly connects to the FAMO treatment of gatekeeping decline, algorithmic curation, the feedback loop, and the practical implications for journalism; it also uses the notion of "affordances as gatekeepers".
- **Keywords**: gatekeeping; algorithmic news curation; automated journalism; news recommender systems; platform power; feedback loops

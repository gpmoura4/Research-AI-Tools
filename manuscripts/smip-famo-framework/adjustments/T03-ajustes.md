```original
Given that this article does not conduct a systematic database sweep, the selection of the three works compared in Section 3 does not result from a documented search-and-screening process, and therefore requires explicit justification. Four criteria guided the selection. First, recency: the three works were published between 2021 2022 and 2023, ensuring the comparison reflects the most current state of the field's synthesis attempts, not historically superseded contributions. Second, ambition of comprehensive synthesis: all three explicitly propose a conceptual architecture covering the relationship between social media and polarization generally, not restricted to a single mechanism or to a specific applied domain — a criterion that excludes both Modgil et al. [1], whose model is developed and validated specifically for the supply-chain management domain, and Tanyel and Windeler [5], whose model tests a single causal pathway (affordance use, interpersonal tension, identity salience) rather than proposing a comprehensive taxonomy. These two works remain central to this article's argument, but as supporting evidence for specific mechanisms and distinctions (Sections 2.3 and 6), not as objects of direct structural comparison.
```

```novo
Because this article does not conduct a systematic database sweep, the three works compared in Section 3 were selected purposively, not through a documented search-and-screening process. The selection is therefore stated here as explicit eligibility criteria, applied both to the selected works and to the most prominent syntheses that were not selected. A work was eligible for structural comparison if it met four criteria. First, scope: it addresses the relationship between social media and polarization in general, rather than a single mechanism, platform, or applied domain. Second, type of contribution: it proposes an explicit conceptual architecture, with named components and stated relations among them, that can be compared with the proposed framework along the dimensions of Table 2. Third, publication: it is a peer-reviewed journal article or conference paper published in English. Fourth, reception: it was published by 2023, which allowed at least two years of scholarly reception before this article was written. When the same research group published more than one synthesis, the most comprehensive one was compared.

SPIR [2], Arora et al. [3], and Tölle and Trier [4] meet all four criteria. The first criterion excludes Modgil et al. [1], whose model is developed and validated specifically for the supply-chain management domain, and Tanyel and Windeler [5], whose model tests a single causal pathway (affordance use, interpersonal tension, identity salience) rather than proposing a comprehensive taxonomy. These two works remain central to this article's argument, but as supporting evidence for specific mechanisms and distinctions (Sections 2.3 and 6), not as objects of direct structural comparison.
```

**Justificativa**: T03-F01, F04 (D1, D4, D5). Os critérios passam a ser de elegibilidade, a priori e aplicáveis a qualquer obra: escopo, tipo de contribuição, publicação e recepção. A recência por janela (que incluía "2022" do T01-A9) sai, junto com "the most current state". O critério de escopo mantém as exclusões de Modgil [1] e Tanyel e Windeler [5] com o mesmo texto. O parágrafo inteiro muda porque a lógica muda.

|---|---|

| Ação | substituir-paragrafo |

```original
Third, diversity of disciplinary tradition: the three works originate from distinct research communities — political psychology and communication, in the case of SPIR [2]; marketing and management, in the case of Arora et al. [3]; information systems, in the case of Tölle and Trier [4] — which strengthens the argument, developed in Section 1, that the structural convergence among them is not an artifact of a single theoretical tradition, but a broader signal of the field's maturation. Fourth, mutual traceability: the three works cite or discuss one another — Tölle and Trier [4] engage directly with Arora et al. [3] within their own review — indicating that they are already recognized, within the literature, as relevant references to one another, and not an arbitrary selection by this article.
```

```novo
Citation impact was deliberately not used as a criterion. The comparison in Section 3 is structural, so it requires works that contain the layers being compared, however often they are cited. For this reason, some of the most cited syntheses in this domain were not compared. Kubin and von Sikorski [110] and Lorenz-Spreen et al. [111] are systematic reviews of empirical evidence. The former examines how (social) media relate to ideological and affective polarization, organizing the evidence by media content, exposure, and effects. The latter examines how digital media relate to a broad set of democratic variables, of which polarization is one, and explicitly sets aside the psychological mechanisms that may shape polarization on social media. Neither proposes an architecture comparable along the dimensions of Table 2, and both inform this article instead as maps of the empirical breadth of the field (Section 4.1). Tucker et al. [112] and Barberá [113] offer influential overviews, but as a report prepared for a foundation and a book chapter, respectively, they fall outside the third criterion. Finally, the short article by Van Bavel et al. [114] comes from the same research group as SPIR [2], the group's most comprehensive synthesis and therefore the one compared here.

The three selected works are thus not presented as a statistical sample of the field, but as architectural syntheses that meet these criteria and originate from three distinct research communities: political psychology and communication, in the case of SPIR [2], marketing and management, in the case of Arora et al. [3], and information systems, in the case of Tölle and Trier [4]. The comparative claims of this article (Sections 1 and 3) refer to these three works. Their structural convergence is also largely independent. SPIR [2] neither cites nor is cited by the other two works, and Arora et al. [3] cite neither of them. Only Tölle and Trier [4] engage directly with Arora et al. [3], which they include among the reviews analyzed in their corpus. That works which largely did not build on one another arrive at similar architectures suggests, without proving it, that this convergence reflects the structure of the problem rather than a shared line of citation.
```

**Justificativa**: T03-F01 a F04 (D2, D3). Responde "por que não X?" nomeando as sínteses mais citadas e o critério que as exclui, conferidos nos PDFs: Kubin e von Sikorski (pp. 190, 193–196, organização por content/exposure/effects); Lorenz-Spreen et al. (p. 75, "we did not focus on the microscopic psychological mechanisms that could shape polarization on social media"); Tucker et al. (p. 1, relatório preparado para a Hewlett Foundation). Van Bavel et al. e Barberá só por metadados (autoria, tamanho, tipo). Diversidade disciplinar vira descrição. "Mutual traceability" sai e dá lugar ao dado real de independência (T03-F02).
